# Visual direction and asset briefs

All assets below are **proposed**. No images, screenshots, diagrams, or slide files have been produced. The briefs specify what a later build should make and how to keep illustrations distinct from evidence.

## Design proposal

Use a quiet reference-desk atmosphere: warm off-white background, dark ink text, teal for the question/response route, and muted amber for checking or uncertainty. These are proposed roles, not approved brand colors. Use a familiar sans-serif typeface such as Aptos or Arial, subject to availability. Explain status in words and symbols as well as color.

Build for 16:9, with a 1280 × 720 reference canvas. Starting targets: 40–44 pt titles, 26–30 pt ordinary copy, and at least 22 pt table text in a PowerPoint build. Treat these as initial design targets and check actual rendered size. Keep diagrams and tables editable. Use simple cuts/reveals, no continuous motion, and provide a static view of every reveal. Put detailed evidence and caveats in notes, while keeping “intended,” “illustration,” and material status limits visible.

Use one main composition per slide. The few tables carry comparisons; images carry the human task and analogy; diagrams carry relationships and sequence. Keep text outside raster images so it remains readable and editable. Do not add official marks or logos until approved assets are supplied.

## V01 — A familiar search

**Slides:** S01. **Visual job:** make the information-finding task recognizable before naming technology.

**Composition:** horizontal image, 3:2 crop on the right half of the slide; person and screen toward the right edge, quiet background on the left. Show a colleague seated at a desk with a small number of manuals and a laptop. No real documents, names, badges, logos, readable equipment labels, or active laboratory procedure.

**Proposed image brief:** “Editorial illustration of an adult laboratory colleague at a reference desk, looking between a laptop and several generic equipment manuals. Warm restrained palette, natural posture, focused rather than frustrated expression, generous negative space on the left, no readable text, no logos, no patient or employee information, no experiment or equipment operation. Horizontal composition.”

**Provenance:** commission/generate a conceptual illustration later or use a permission-cleared photograph. Record origin and license. Do not call it a photograph of our lab. **Visible disclosure:** conceptual illustration, if the visual could be mistaken for an observed setting.

**Alt text:** “A colleague looks between a laptop and equipment manuals while trying to locate information.” **Fallback:** a large running question beside a generic manual illustration. **Crop check:** face, hands, and manuals remain recognizable; no tiny text is necessary to interpret the scene.

## V02 — Predict the first reply

**Slides:** S02. **Visual job:** show a conversation that can clarify before offering information.

**Composition:** three frames across the slide: question, proposed reply, source verification. Use the exact S02 copy. Reveal the second frame only after the audience predicts a response. The proposed reply is a clarifying question, not invented cleaning guidance. The third frame depicts the later source-check action without implying retrieval has already succeeded.

**Production:** editable explanatory diagram with simple message outlines and document symbol, or separate sourced/generated conceptual vignettes with native text. Do not recreate EDAV interface controls from imagination. No “live demo,” success indicator, invented manual identifier, or page number.

**Provenance:** original teaching illustration informed by the required behavior in E03/E04. **Visible disclosure:** “Illustration of intended behavior.”

**Alt text:** “A person asks where cleaning information is located; the proposed bot reply asks which instrument and model; the person later checks supporting manual content.” **Fallback:** the native table in S02, fully visible. **Reveal check:** reading order remains question → clarification → check in the static version.

## V03 — One reference library

**Slides:** S05. **Visual job:** explain shared service while showing that the project team still contributes.

**Composition:** one editorial library scene, 16:9 background or 3:2 central crop, with five external text labels. A shelf represents manuals; a reference desk sign represents the conversational helper; an access terminal represents the app. EDAV and project-team contributions appear as plain labels beside the scene, not invented corporate logos. Avoid showing an autonomous robot doing laboratory work.

**Proposed image brief:** “A calm modern reference library, a small collection of generic technical manuals, an unstaffed help desk, and a simple access terminal. Restrained editorial style, warm paper and teal accents, clear negative space for five labels to be added later. No text, logos, robot, glowing brain, or fantasy technology. Do not imply a human expert is inside the computer.”

**Provenance:** conceptual illustration to be created later; record asset source. **Visible disclosure:** retain the S05 statement that the helper is software and can make mistakes. Explain the analogy's limits aloud.

**Alt text:** “A reference library represents the manual collection, conversational helper, app access, shared technology, and project responsibilities.” **Fallback:** five plain role labels in a single horizontal composition. **Legibility check:** the audience can map each role without deciphering details inside the artwork.

## V04 — Answer to supporting passage

**Slides:** S08. **Visual job:** show what a checkable source relationship looks like.

**Composition:** three connected areas: answer outline, source identity, supporting passage. An enlarged detail makes the relationship visible. Use only these placeholder labels: “Answer based on evidence,” “Manual identified here,” and “Supporting passage shown here.” Keep the three actual checking questions from S08 visible.

**Production:** editable evidence diagram. Keep the placeholder passage blank except for its explanatory label. If a real passage is later approved, verify exact wording, model, document revision, relevant conditions, and permitted disclosure before replacement. Use only source locators actually observed; never fill in an illustrative page number.

**Provenance:** original conceptual diagram; any later real excerpt requires its own source and review record. **Visible disclosure:** “Illustration; no live answer shown.”

**Alt text:** “An answer is connected to the manual identity and the passage that a person would inspect to check support.” **Fallback:** three checking questions beside a generic document outline. **Evidence check:** the highlighted passage actually supports the displayed claim if real content replaces the placeholder; decorative highlighting alone is insufficient.

## V05 — Two ways to reach a source check

**Slides:** S12. **Visual job:** compare the proposed experience with an illustrative existing search, without inventing gains.

**Composition:** two horizontal paths of equal visual weight and similar length. The upper path is labeled “Illustrative search”; the lower is “Proposed experience.” Both end at a person reviewing a manual. Use the exact S12 labels.

**Production:** editable comparison diagram. No stopwatch, percentage, speed arrow, “before/after results,” or path-length encoding of time. No implication that all users currently search in the same way.

**Provenance:** teaching scenario and proposal, not a measured workflow study. **Visible disclosure:** “The pilot will test whether this helps.”

**Alt text:** “One route opens manuals and searches; the proposed route starts with a question and clarification. Both finish with a person checking the source.” **Fallback:** two labeled text lines and the closing takeaway. **Interpretation check:** a viewer cannot mistake the figure for evidence of a measured improvement.

## V06 — The actual pieces and the two moments

**Slides:** S06 and S07. **Visual job:** distinguish relationships from sequence. These are two different diagrams, not repeated decorative imagery.

**V06a, S06 relationship map:** person on left; app outline containing EDAV chat in center; EDAV service outline containing EM Knowledge Bot on right; approved collection below. Label the collection connector “Manuals to consult” and response connector “Reply and source information.” Add an explicit source-check action for the person. Do not locate manual storage on a particular server. **Alt text:** “A person interacts with EDAV chat embedded in the app; the intended bot arrangement uses the approved collection and returns information for the person to check.”

**V06b, S07 process map:** top lane “Before use,” bottom lane “For a question.” Use S07's exact labels. Add an alternate branch for missing evidence. Use solid lines for the conceptual sequence and text “Intended process”; do not suggest line style alone proves implementation. **Alt text:** “Manual selection and preparation happen before use. A later question prompts clarification, passage finding, an evidence-based draft, source display, and human checking, or an explanation that evidence is missing.”

**Production:** explicitly editable native diagrams. **Provenance:** E02 supports embedding; E03/E04 define the intended evidence behavior. Internal retrieval implementation is unverified. **Fallback:** S06 and S07's text sequences. **Accuracy check:** distinguish a verified integration contract from an intended live experience.

## Future visual review

Inspect every slide at presentation size, every reveal in order, and the static/print version. Check wrapping, table density, contrast, alt text, and the visibility of intended/illustrative status. Ask a nontechnical colleague to explain S06 without reading the notes. Structural checks cannot establish visual clarity; no visual QA has been claimed for this planning-only package.
